## 2. CURRICULUM ALIGNMENT (Level 3–4 English, Years 5–6, NZ Curriculum)

**Listening, Reading, and Viewing**
- *Ideas:* Show understanding of ideas within, across, and beyond texts.
- *Structure and Language:* Show understanding of how texts are shaped for different purposes and audiences.
- *Processes and Strategies:* Integrate sources of information, processes, and strategies confidently to identify, form, and express ideas.

**Speaking, Writing, and Presenting**
- *Ideas:* Select, form, and communicate ideas on a range of topics.
- *Structure and Language:* Show understanding of language features, using them appropriately.
- *Processes and Strategies:* Select and use processes and strategies to communicate confidently.

**Key Competencies:** Relating to Others, Thinking, Managing Self, Participating & Contributing

**Values this unit explores:** Kindness across difference, resilience/adaptability, belonging and found family, courage and self-sacrifice.

**Cross-curricular link:** Science — Living World (adaptation to environment, seasons, survival) and the Nature of Science strand (technology and the natural world interacting), since Roz the robot must learn to survive using the same ecological pressures — food, shelter, seasons, predators — that drive every wild creature on the island. Also a natural link to Technology (Digital Technologies / Design & Innovation) in discussing what makes Roz "alive-like" versus how real robots and AI are built and used.

| **Wednesday** | Teacher reads aloud | Same format as Monday, next reading chunk. |

| **Friday** | Quiz day | 20-question quiz over that week's four chunks. Auto-marked; printable version with answer key. |

Each week also has one rotating menu of 4 projects (poster / comic strip / drama-acting scene / journal reflection), each referencing that week's actual events, done in spare/extension time.

## 5. DETAILED WEEKLY CONTENT

### WEEK 1 — Chapters 1–10: "Washed Ashore"

**Learning Intention:** We are learning to infer a character's feelings from their actions when the text doesn't say those feelings directly.
**Success Criteria:** I can point to what a character *does* and use it to explain how they might be feeling, even when the author doesn't tell me.

**Monday — Chapters 1–2 (The storm; Roz washes ashore)**
- Lead-in: If you woke up somewhere completely unfamiliar, with no memory of how you got there, what's the very first thing you'd try to do?
- Comprehension: What destroys the crate Roz is packed in, and how does she end up activated on the island?
- Inference: The chapter doesn't say Roz is "scared" — what details suggest she is uncertain or cautious in her first moments?
- Go-away: Write two diary-style sentences as Roz, describing your first sight of the island.

**Tuesday — Chapters 3–5 (Roz wakes properly; first movements; watching the animals)**
- Lead-in: Have you ever had to learn something just by copying someone else, with no instructions? What was it?
- Comprehension: How does Roz first try to move and communicate, and what goes wrong?
- Inference: Why do you think the animals react with fear rather than curiosity when they see Roz?
- Go-away: List three things Roz would need to learn to survive on the island, in order of urgency.

**Wednesday — Chapters 6–8 (First contact with island animals; being called "the monster")**
- Lead-in: If you were the very first of your kind that anyone had ever seen, how would you want to be treated?
- Comprehension: What nickname do the island's animals give Roz, and why?
- Inference: What does the animals' reaction reveal about how the island community treats anything unfamiliar?
- Go-away: Describe the island from a small animal's point of view, seeing Roz for the first time.

**Thursday — Chapters 9–10 (Roz begins observing and mimicking animal sounds)**
- Lead-in: What's the hardest part of trying to fit in somewhere brand new?
- Comprehension: What method does Roz use to start understanding the animals around her?
- Inference: Why might learning to "sound like" the island's creatures matter more to Roz's survival than learning to look like them?
- Go-away: Roz is designed to adapt to any environment. Write about one way a person you know has had to adapt to a big change.

**Weekly Project Menu**
1. **Poster:** Design a "WANTED — Have You Seen This Creature?" poster the island animals might make about Roz, based only on rumours, not facts.
2. **Comic strip:** 6 panels showing Roz's first day on the island from her activation to the animals fleeing from her.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Act out the animals' meeting where they decide what to do about "the monster."
4. **Journal reflection:** Write Roz's first log entry, in her own robotic, logical voice, describing what she has learned so far.

**Friday Quiz — Week 1 (20 questions)**
1. What kind of event causes Roz's crate to break open? — *A storm/shipwreck.*
2. True or False: Roz remembers exactly how she arrived on the island. — *False.*
3. What is Roz designed to do, according to her manufacturer? — *Adapt to and survive in any environment.*
4. Which sense does Roz rely on most to begin learning about the island? — *Observation/sight and sound.*
5. What do the island animals nickname Roz? — *"The monster" (accept close equivalents used in your edition).*
6. Why are the animals afraid of Roz at first? — *She's unfamiliar, large, and makes strange sounds/movements.*
7. Short answer: Name one way Roz tries to communicate in her early chapters. — *Mimicking animal sounds/movements.*
8. Vocabulary: What does "unfamiliar" mean? — *Not known or recognised; strange.*
9. What is Roz's first major challenge on the island? — *Learning to move/function and understand her surroundings.*
10. True or False: The animals welcome Roz warmly straight away. — *False.*
11. In your own words: How does Roz's approach to learning differ from how a human child might learn? — *Open-ended — logical, observational, systematic repetition, no fear response the way an animal might have.*
12. What happens to the crate/packaging Roz arrived in? — *It is destroyed/lost in the storm.*
13. Which of these best describes the island: rainforest, wild uninhabited island, city park? — *Wild uninhabited island.*
14. Why might the author choose to have Roz "wake up" with no memory of arriving? — *To mirror the reader's own confusion and build sympathy for Roz as an outsider.*
15. Inference: What does Roz's persistence in these chapters suggest about her character? — *She is determined/logical/doesn't give up despite setbacks.*
16. Short answer: What is one risk Roz faces simply by existing on the island? — *Being attacked/rejected by animals, or environmental damage (rocks, weather).*
17. True or False: The animals immediately understand that Roz means no harm. — *False.*
18. In your own words: Why do you think first impressions matter so much in this story so far? — *Open-ended — they shape how Roz is treated and whether she can survive there.*
19. What does Roz begin practising in Chapters 9–10 to try to fit in? — *Mimicking/copying animal sounds and behaviours.*
20. In your own words: What is one prediction you have for how Roz's relationship with the island animals might change? — *Open-ended, accept any reasonable, text-supported prediction.*

### WEEK 2 — Chapters 11–19: "Learning the Island's Ways"

**Learning Intention:** We are learning to track how a character changes over several chapters by noticing small shifts in their actions.
**Success Criteria:** I can compare how a character behaves at the start of a section with how they behave by the end, and explain what caused the change.

**Monday — Chapters 11–12 (Roz begins to understand animal language)**
- Lead-in: Imagine trying to learn a new language just by listening — no book, no teacher. What would be the hardest part?
- Comprehension: How does Roz's understanding of the animals' sounds begin to improve?
- Inference: What does it show about Roz that she keeps trying even though the animals keep avoiding her?
- Go-away: Invent three animal "words" Roz might learn this week, and what they mean.

**Tuesday — Chapters 13–15 (Small, cautious interactions begin; Pinktail-type early contact)**
- Lead-in: Has an animal (pet or wild) ever surprised you by trusting you when you didn't expect it to?
- Comprehension: Which animal is the first to cautiously interact with Roz, and how does that interaction go?
- Inference: Why might one animal be braver than the rest of the island community?
- Go-away: Write a short conversation (in speech bubbles or dialogue) between Roz and the first animal that talks to her.

**Wednesday — Chapters 16–17 (Roz begins to be tolerated, still an outsider)**
- Lead-in: What's something that made you feel like you belonged somewhere, even a little?
- Comprehension: How does the island community's attitude toward Roz start to shift, even slightly, in these chapters?
- Inference: Is the island's changing opinion of Roz based on trust, or just familiarity? Explain your thinking.
- Go-away: Describe one small act that could change how a group of people sees a newcomer.

**Thursday — Chapters 18–19 (Roz builds early routines and begins to feel more at home)**
- Lead-in: What helps you feel settled in a new place — a routine, a friend, a familiar object?
- Comprehension: What routines or habits does Roz start to build for herself on the island?
- Inference: Why might having a routine matter just as much to a robot as it would to a person?
- Go-away: Design a daily routine for Roz, based on what she needs to survive and what she's learned so far.

**Weekly Project Menu**
1. **Poster:** Create an "Island Field Guide" page introducing Roz to a new animal arriving on the island, written from another animal's point of view.
2. **Comic strip:** Show the moment the first animal dares to approach Roz, panel by panel.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Act out a cautious first conversation between Roz and one of the island's animals.
4. **Journal reflection:** Write a log entry from Roz describing what she has learned about the island's "rules" so far.

**Friday Quiz — Week 2 (20 questions)**
1. How does Roz begin to understand the animals? — *By listening carefully and studying patterns in their sounds/behaviour.*
2. True or False: Every animal on the island quickly becomes friendly with Roz. — *False.*
3. Which animal is among the first to interact more closely with Roz? — *Accept the first animal your edition introduces as more curious/cautiously friendly.*
4. Vocabulary: What does "cautious" mean? — *Careful, not taking risks.*
5. Short answer: Name one routine or habit Roz starts to build in these chapters. — *Open — e.g. observing the island each day, practising sounds.*
6. Why does Roz keep trying to connect with the animals despite being avoided? — *She is built to adapt/survive, and needs the island community to do so.*
7. True or False: Roz gives up trying to communicate after the animals avoid her. — *False.*
8. In your own words: What does the growing trust between Roz and the animals suggest about first impressions? — *Open-ended — first impressions can change with time and effort.*
9. What does Roz do to try to prove she isn't dangerous? — *Open — behaves gently, mimics friendly behaviour, avoids conflict.*
10. Inference: What might make an animal community slowly accept an outsider? — *Repeated safe/positive interactions, time, no threat shown.*
11. Short answer: What is one "rule" of island life Roz starts to learn in these chapters? — *Open — e.g. who eats what, where danger lies, seasonal patterns.*
12. Vocabulary: What does "tolerate" mean, as opposed to "welcome"? — *Tolerate = allow without objecting; welcome = actively invite/embrace.*
13. True or False: By the end of Chapter 19, every animal on the island fully trusts Roz. — *False — trust is only beginning.*
14. In your own words: How is Roz's situation similar to what a new student might feel on their first weeks at a new school? — *Open-ended.*
15. What does Roz rely on since she cannot rely on instinct the way animals do? — *Observation, logic, learning/adaptation.*
16. Short answer: Describe one moment in these chapters where an animal shows the smallest sign of trust toward Roz. — *Open, text-based.*
17. Why is the island's response to Roz realistic, in your opinion? — *Open-ended — accept reasoned answers about fear of the unknown.*
18. In your own words: What would you have done differently if you were Roz, trying to be accepted? — *Open-ended.*
19. True or False: Roz begins forming routines to help her survive and understand the island better. — *True.*
20. Predict: What do you think might happen that finally forces the animals to see Roz differently? — *Open-ended, text-supported prediction (accept references to the coming accident).*

### WEEK 3 — Chapters 20–28: "The Last Egg"

**Learning Intention:** We are learning to explore how an accident or mistake can change the direction of a whole story.
**Success Criteria:** I can explain how one event causes a character to make a decision that changes everything that follows.

**Monday — Chapters 20–21 (The accident — Roz stumbles into the goose nest)**
- Lead-in: Have you ever made a mistake that you couldn't take back, even though you didn't mean to cause harm?
- Comprehension: What happens to the goose family when Roz loses her footing on the cliffside?
- Inference: How do you think Roz "feels" (in her own robotic way) immediately after the accident, based on what she does next?
- Go-away: Write two sentences from Roz's internal processing/log right after the accident happens.

**Tuesday — Chapters 22–23 (Guilt; discovering the one surviving egg)**
- Lead-in: If you accidentally broke something that mattered to someone else, what would you want to do to make it right?
- Comprehension: What does Roz find among the wreckage of the nest, and what does she decide to do about it?
- Inference: Why might Roz feel a sense of responsibility for the egg, even though robots aren't supposed to feel guilt?
- Go-away: Write a short apology, in Roz's voice, to the goose family she can no longer speak to.

**Wednesday — Chapters 24–26 (Deciding to raise the egg; Fink's role)**
- Lead-in: Would you trust advice from someone you don't fully like or agree with? Why or why not?
- Comprehension: Who helps Roz figure out how to care for the egg, and what is that character's personality like?
- Inference: Why might the author choose a sly, self-interested character to be the one who helps Roz?
- Go-away: List three things Roz will need to learn in order to keep the egg safe and help it hatch.

**Thursday — Chapters 27–28 (The egg hatches — Brightbill imprints on Roz)**
- Lead-in: What do you think it means to be someone's "family," if you don't look anything alike?
- Comprehension: What happens the moment the egg hatches, and how does the gosling react to Roz?
- Inference: Why does the gosling accept Roz as his mother, even though she is a robot?
- Go-away: Write the first "thought" the newly hatched gosling might have upon seeing Roz.

**Weekly Project Menu**
1. **Poster:** Design a "before and after" split poster showing the nest before the accident and Roz caring for the surviving egg.
2. **Comic strip:** Show the hatching moment and Brightbill's first sight of Roz, from Brightbill's point of view.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Act out the conversation between Roz and Fink where he agrees to help her understand goslings.
4. **Journal reflection:** Write about a time you or someone you know took responsibility for a mistake and tried to fix it.

**Friday Quiz — Week 3 (20 questions)**
1. What causes the accident with the goose nest? — *Roz loses her balance/footing and falls, destroying the nest.*
2. True or False: All of the goose eggs survive the accident. — *False, only one survives.*
3. What does Roz decide to do with the surviving egg? — *Keep and care for it/try to hatch it.*
4. Who helps guide Roz in caring for the egg? — *Fink the fox (accept the equivalent character name from your edition).*
5. Vocabulary: What does "responsibility" mean? — *Being accountable for the outcome of your actions.*
6. Short answer: Why might Fink actually be willing to help Roz? — *Open — self-interest, curiosity, or a growing, reluctant fondness.*
7. True or False: Roz feels nothing at all after causing the accident. — *False — she shows guilt/responsibility through her actions.*
8. What happens the moment the gosling hatches and sees Roz? — *He imprints on her, treating her as his mother.*
9. Inference: Why does the gosling accept a robot as his mother? — *Because imprinting is based on what a gosling sees first, not species/appearance.*
10. In your own words: Do you think Roz caring for the egg makes up for the accident? Why or why not? — *Open-ended.*
11. Short answer: What is one thing Roz must learn quickly in order to protect the egg? — *Open — warmth, safety from predators, patience.*
12. Vocabulary: What does "imprint" mean in this context? — *To form an early, strong attachment/bond, usually to the first moving thing seen after hatching.*
13. True or False: Fink is immediately kind and helpful with no hesitation. — *False — he is initially reluctant/self-interested.*
14. In your own words: What does this section suggest about family not needing to "match"? — *Open-ended.*
15. What does Roz name the gosling (or is he named later)? — *Accept whatever your edition establishes — if not yet named, "not yet named" is acceptable.*
16. Short answer: Describe how Roz's role changes across this week's chapters. — *From accidental cause of harm to caretaker/mother figure.*
17. Why might the author want readers to feel sympathy for Roz here, right after she causes harm? — *Open-ended — shows her capacity for care/responsibility despite the accident.*
18. In your own words: How is Fink different from the other animals we've met so far? — *Open — more cunning, self-serving, but ultimately useful/loyal.*
19. True or False: The gosling could have chosen to imprint on another animal instead of Roz. — *True (in theory) — but he imprinted on Roz because she was there first.*
20. Predict: How do you think the other geese will react to Brightbill being raised by a robot? — *Open-ended, text-supported prediction.*

### WEEK 4 — Chapters 29–37: "Learning to Mother"

**Learning Intention:** We are learning to identify how an author shows a character learning a new skill through trial and error.
**Success Criteria:** I can find examples in the text where a character fails, adjusts, and tries again.

**Monday — Chapters 29–30 (Roz figures out feeding and basic care)**
- Lead-in: What's something you had to fail at a few times before you got it right?
- Comprehension: What early mistakes does Roz make while learning to care for the gosling?
- Inference: How does Roz's logical, methodical nature help her (or get in the way) as a new mother?
- Go-away: Write a short "user manual" page Roz might write for herself titled "How to Care for a Gosling."

**Tuesday — Chapters 31–33 (Other geese reject/tease Brightbill for having a robot mother)**
- Lead-in: Has anyone ever teased you or someone you know for being "different"? How did that feel?
- Comprehension: How do the other geese react to Brightbill because of who is raising him?
- Inference: Why do you think the geese focus on Roz being "different" rather than on how well she cares for Brightbill?
- Go-away: Write a message of support you would give Brightbill if you were his friend.

**Wednesday — Chapters 34–35 (Roz problem-solves parenting challenges with the island's help)**
- Lead-in: When something feels too hard to do alone, who do you ask for help?
- Comprehension: Which island animals start pitching in to help Roz raise Brightbill, and how?
- Inference: What does the community's willingness to help suggest about how far Roz has come since Week 1?
- Go-away: List three ways the island community now treats Roz differently than they did in Week 1.

**Thursday — Chapters 36–37 (The bond between Roz and Brightbill deepens)**
- Lead-in: What's a small moment that made you feel close to someone in your family?
- Comprehension: Describe a specific moment in these chapters that shows how close Roz and Brightbill have become.
- Inference: Why might small, everyday moments matter more to a growing bond than big dramatic ones?
- Go-away: Write about a small, everyday moment that shows love or care in your own life.

**Weekly Project Menu**

3. **Drama/acting scene:** Act out the other geese teasing Brightbill, and how he responds.

**Friday Quiz — Week 4 (20 questions)**

2. True or False: Roz gets everything right immediately as a new mother. — *False.*
3. How do the other geese treat Brightbill because of his robot mother? — *They tease/reject/exclude him.*
4. Vocabulary: What does "reject" mean? — *To refuse to accept or include.*
5. Short answer: Name one island animal that starts helping Roz raise Brightbill. — *Open, accept text-supported answer.*
6. Why might Roz's logical approach sometimes clash with what a gosling actually needs? — *Open-ended — instincts/emotions vs. pure logic.*
7. True or False: Roz never adjusts her methods, even when they don't work. — *False — she learns and adapts through trial and error.*
8. Inference: Why do you think Brightbill stays loyal to Roz even when teased by other geese? — *Open — she is the one who cares for/loves him regardless.*
9. In your own words: What does this section suggest about what "family" really means? — *Open-ended — care and bond matter more than sameness/appearance.*
10. Short answer: Describe one small, tender moment between Roz and Brightbill from these chapters. — *Open, text-based.*
11. Vocabulary: What does "bond" mean in the context of a relationship? — *A close connection or attachment between individuals.*
12. True or False: The island community is completely unwilling to help Roz with Brightbill. — *False — some animals start helping.*
13. In your own words: How has the island's view of Roz changed since Week 1? — *Open-ended, but should reference growing trust/acceptance.*
14. Why might the author include the other geese's teasing rather than skip straight to acceptance? — *Open-ended — shows realistic social struggle, raises the emotional stakes.*

17. What quality does Roz show throughout this week that helps her succeed as a mother? — *Open — patience, persistence, adaptability, care.*
18. True or False: Brightbill begins to trust and rely on Roz more as the weeks go on. — *True.*

### WEEK 5 — Chapters 38–46: "Finding Her Place"

**Learning Intention:** We are learning to notice how setting (season, weather, environment) reflects or affects a character's mood and situation.
**Success Criteria:** I can describe how a change in setting connects to what a character is going through at that point in the story.

**Monday — Chapters 38–39 (Summer arrives; island life in full swing)**
- Lead-in: What does your favourite season feel like, and why do you love it?
- Comprehension: How is island life described during summer in these chapters?
- Inference: Why might summer be a natural "high point" for Roz and Brightbill's story so far?
- Go-away: Describe a summer memory of your own that made you feel happy or free.

**Tuesday — Chapters 40–42 (Roz helps solve problems for other animals; becomes useful to the community)**
- Lead-in: What's a skill or strength of yours that helps other people?
- Comprehension: What problem(s) does Roz help solve for other island animals in these chapters?
- Inference: How does helping others change how the island community sees Roz?
- Go-away: Invent one more problem Roz could help an island animal solve, and explain how she'd do it.

**Wednesday — Chapters 43–44 (Roz teaches Brightbill to swim/forage)**
- Lead-in: What's something an adult in your life taught you how to do?
- Comprehension: What survival skills does Roz teach Brightbill in these chapters?
- Inference: Why is it important that Roz teaches Brightbill skills a goose actually needs, even though she isn't a goose herself?
- Go-away: Write step-by-step instructions Roz might give Brightbill for one of these skills.

**Thursday — Chapters 45–46 (Brightbill's first, clumsy attempts at flight)**
- Lead-in: Describe a time you tried something new and it didn't go smoothly at first.
- Comprehension: What happens when Brightbill first tries to fly?
- Inference: How does Roz react to Brightbill's failed attempts, and what does that reveal about her as a parent?
- Go-away: Write an encouraging note from Roz to Brightbill after his first failed flying attempt.

**Weekly Project Menu**
1. **Poster:** Create a "Roz's Island Services" poster listing the ways Roz now helps the island community.
2. **Comic strip:** Show Brightbill's first flying attempt going wrong, panel by panel, ending on a hopeful note.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Act out Roz teaching Brightbill a survival skill, in role as teacher and student.
4. **Journal reflection:** Write about a time someone encouraged you after you failed at something the first time.

**Friday Quiz — Week 5 (20 questions)**
1. What season is it during these chapters? — *Summer.*
2. True or False: Roz has become fully rejected by the island community by this point. — *False — she is now helping and becoming accepted.*
3. Name one problem Roz helps an island animal solve. — *Open, text-based.*
4. Vocabulary: What does "forage" mean? — *To search for and find food.*
5. Short answer: What survival skill(s) does Roz teach Brightbill in these chapters? — *Open — swimming, foraging, etc.*
6. Why is it notable that Roz, a robot, can teach a goose to do goose things? — *She learned these skills herself through observation/adaptation.*
7. True or False: Brightbill flies perfectly on his very first attempt. — *False.*
8. Inference: How does Roz respond when Brightbill's flying attempt fails? — *Open — supportive, encouraging, patient.*
9. In your own words: Why might summer be shown as a peaceful, happy time in the story? — *Open-ended — contrast with hardships before/after; reflects growing stability.*
10. Short answer: Give one piece of evidence that the island community now values Roz's presence. — *Open, text-based.*
11. Vocabulary: What does "community" mean in the context of this story? — *A group of individuals living/working together and supporting one another.*

## 6. WEEK 10 — FINAL PROJECT (3 lessons) + Presentation & Celebration

No new reading. Students apply everything they've learned about *The Wild Robot*.

1. **A "Found Family" campaign project** — students create a campaign (posters, slogans, a short speech) celebrating the idea that family is built through care, not sameness, using Roz and Brightbill's story as the model.
2. **An extended drama/performance scene** — either imagining Brightbill's journey to find Roz after the book ends, or dramatising a moment from the RECOs' point of view, told with empathy rather than as simple "villains."
3. **A character case file** — an in-depth profile (poster + written report + panels) tracking Roz's journey and change across the whole book, with evidence from every part of the story she appears in.
4. **An original class anthology/book** inspired by the book's core theme — each student contributes one page titled "How I Adapted," a real-life story (illustrated) of a time they adapted to change, echoing Roz's own journey of adaptation.

## 8. IMAGE PROMPT LIBRARY

**Chosen visual identity:** a warm **forest-meets-circuitry** palette — mossy greens, soft driftwood browns, warm copper/bronze (echoing Roz's metal body), and pale sky blue, with a recurring motif of simple circuit-line patterns growing like vines through leaves and branches — symbolising a robot slowly becoming part of the natural world.
